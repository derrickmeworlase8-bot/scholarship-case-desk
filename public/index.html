Contact your school directly

The single most reliable way to check a scholarship: call or email your school using a number or address you found on your school's own official website — never one provided in the scholarship offer itself.

Field guide

Common red flags

  • Any fee to "apply," "process," or "release" your award — legitimate scholarships never charge you.
  • Requests for a bank account, SSN, or card number before you've even been chosen.
  • "You've already won" or "guaranteed" language with no actual application.
  • Pressure to respond within hours or the offer "expires."
  • Payment demanded by wire transfer, gift card, or payment app.
  • Vague sponsor name with no verifiable school, foundation, or company behind it.
  • Contact only through a personal email address rather than an institutional one.

Signs of the real thing

  • A real application: essay, transcript, recommendation letters, or a form with a clear deadline.
  • A named, searchable sponsor — a school, company, foundation, or civic group with an independent web presence.
  • Contact from an institutional domain (.edu, a school district, or the sponsor's own verified domain).
  • Selection criteria you can read in advance, not just "everyone qualifies."
  • Funds paid to your school directly, or through a standard, traceable method.
  • Your counselor, financial aid office, or a search engine turns up independent mentions of it.

If you've spotted a scam

Report it to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov (US), the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (Canada), or Action Fraud (UK) — and let your school's financial aid office know so they can warn other students.

If you already shared bank or card details, contact your bank immediately to flag possible fraud.

Screening signals only — not legal or financial certainty. Always verify directly with the school or sponsor before paying anything or sharing personal information.
